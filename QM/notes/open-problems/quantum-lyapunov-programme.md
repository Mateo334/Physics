## Settled obstructions and corrected claims
- [Proved] Under common unitary, rho and its initial-state tangent V obey the same commutator evolution. Unitarily invariant full-state tangent norms are constant at every time. Fixed bounded readout differences are bounded by ||A|| times the initial trace norm; outcome total variation is bounded by the initial trace distance. Finite-time amplification from a small initial visible component remains possible.
- [Proved] Retaining rho resolves the need for autonomous readout closure, not the interpretation of growth. Hidden tangents becoming visible make unrestricted final/initial readout gain infinite; a periodic qubit does this. Complete coordinates with the inherited metric repeat isometry. [Derivations and exact criteria](../theorems/observable-sensitivity-closed-dynamics.md).
- [Correction] Linear evolution does NOT itself exclude classical instability: classical Liouville evolution is linear and preserves fine-grained statistical distinguishability too. Physical geometry and preparation scaling matter. Point-mass Wasserstein distance exactly equals trajectory distance; finite-width or coarse distributions require additional analysis.
- [Correction] Neither nonlinear f(rho) nor noninvertibility is the missing instability mechanism. Invertible f is a reparametrization; noninvertible f discards information and can destroy closure. It helps reduction, not necessarily stretching.
- Exact autonomy requires Df_rho[-i[H,rho]/hbar]=F(f(rho)); equal readouts must give equal velocities. If F is smooth, dot(delta x)=DF_x delta x. This does NOT establish a Hamiltonian structure or instability. Spectral functions of rho are autonomous but constant.

## Nonlinear quantum maps and alternative equations
Conditional measurement N_i(rho)=K_i rho K_i†/p_i is generally nonlinear. Its tangent is K_i V K_i†/p_i - N_i(rho) Tr(K_i V K_i†)/p_i. Rank-deficient K_i can make it noninvertible; invertible K_i gives an invertible normalized map. A rank-one outcome sends all successful preparations to the same state and erases the tangent. A proper projector of rank at least two gives a generally nonlinear, noninvertible update. Conditioning can amplify through rare outcomes without Hamiltonian chaos. Repeated conditional trajectories can have meaningful stability, but depend on the instrument, record law and geometry; they answer a different question from isolated dynamics. This was already investigated in the notebook, not a new candidate.

TDVP/mean-field: projecting the quantum vector field onto a variational state family with nondegenerate symplectic form gives Hamiltonian parameter flow. In local canonical coordinates dot z=J grad E and dot(delta z)=J Hess(E) delta z. Projection can allow stretching because it changes effective dynamics, not just coordinates. An exactly invariant family remains isometric in the induced quantum metric. The physical system may be closed while the description is approximate; accuracy of both base and tangent requires checking.
