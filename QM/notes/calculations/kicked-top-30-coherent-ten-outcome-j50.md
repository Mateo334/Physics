PC reports ~34.0 GB physical RAM (about 31.7 GiB) and 20 logical processors; Python 3.13, NumPy 2.3.5, SciPy 1.16.3, Matplotlib 3.10.8. CVXPY unavailable, hence Dykstra Euclidean-projection fallback. Dense 10-effect d=101 per-projection benchmark ~0.15 s (ten sweeps, one BLAS thread); d=151 ~0.41 s in same benchmark. This is a *practical run-size pilot*, **not** a claim that d=101 is the highest dimension the PC can support: no finite hardware-maximum numerical optimization was established.

Model: spin j=50, Hilbert dimension d=101, K=3 and 6. Thirty independently sphere-uniform oriented pure spin-coherent states (seed 20261123), same states for both K; theta/phi saved in JSON. At t0=1.1 (one full Floquet cycle plus 0.1 into second rotation, H=pi Jy) maximize the mean of the 30 separate instantaneous OE derivatives, one *shared* unrestricted ten-outcome POVM for each K. Effects mixed with 10^-6 I/10 after projection to guarantee strict PSD; no mixed states or measurement backaction. Initial POVM seed 20261124. No classical exponents for all thirty directions computed; do not call t0 an established common pre-Ehrenfest window.

**Critical warning:** objective was still increasing sharply at the time cap (figure visibly rising), and residuals are large. These are *feasible lower bounds*, **not reliable optimized maxima or converged rates**. The requested three random restarts did not meaningfully run: the time budget was per K rather than per restart, so only restart 0 made gradient steps; entries 1 and 2 in the saved historical JSON each record a random initial candidate stopped immediately with `time budget`. Subsequently the script was fixed to skip further restarts once budget expires and report actual attempted restarts. Do not describe this as a successful three-start search. Absolute rates cannot be compared to the earlier d=21 ensemble's rates as a size scaling, since both state ensemble and dimension changed.

Checks: coherent-state Jz expectations match j cos(theta) to 1e-8; unitary norm/trace and analytic gradient directional tests (relative errors ~4.7e-9, 2.3e-10); centered h=1e-6 entropy slope checks 72.40505059, 118.07386453; POVM completeness errors 1.7e-15 and 1.5e-15, all final minimum effect eigenvalues >=9.998e-8. The figure was inspected visually and displays nonstationary optimization and nonlinear local entropy near t0. Higher j costs grow steeply with dense eigenvalue PSD projection; a longer budget or scalable algorithm is needed for trustworthy maxima.

[Proved] Because all thirty states remain pure, if each state gets its **own** independently optimized POVM, the average optimized rate equals `C_{101,10} * mean_a ΔH_a`. If a **single common** POVM must work for all, `sup_E mean_a R_a(E) <= C_{101,10} * mean_a ΔH_a`, with equality requiring one POVM to be simultaneously optimal (not established). The unitary-equivalence argument for a single state does not make the common-POVM maximum proportional to the mean uncertainty. With H=pi Jy the measured mean ΔH from saved states is 26.73665791 for K3 and 45.97748799 for K6; the **unconverged attained** R/mean ΔH values are 2.70808 and 2.56808. Their difference does not prove that the true optimum violates any specific proportionality: the optimization residuals are large.
