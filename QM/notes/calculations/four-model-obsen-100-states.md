# One-graph observational-entropy comparison across four models

[Numerically tested] `calculations/code/compare_obsen_four_models_100_states.py` generates `calculations/results/obsen_four_models_100_states.png` (visually inspected). Run from workspace root using `py -3.13 calculations/code/compare_obsen_four_models_100_states.py`. Test environment: Python 3.13.17, NumPy 2.3.5, SciPy 1.16.3, Matplotlib 3.10.8; `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1`, `MPLBACKEND=Agg`. Reproducible base seed 20261116; 100 independent initial coherent states per model, 21 sample times t=0,...,20, one realization each of the disordered models. The plotted mean is the arithmetic mean of 100 *individual* observational entropies divided by `log(d_sector)`, not the observational entropy of the ensemble-averaged density matrix.

Measurements are fixed rank-one occupation-basis PVMs for SYK and Bose--Hubbard and fixed Jz-basis rank-one PVMs for the kicked tops and GOE. For each, `S_obs(psi)=-sum_i |psi_i|^2 log(|psi_i|^2)` since every effect has trace one; no measurement backaction enters the evolution. The observable choices are **not operationally equivalent across models**. The normalization by `log(d_sector)` displays different Hilbert dimensions on one vertical scale; the raw entropies differ by this factor.

- Two kicked tops: spin j=20, d=41, Floquet `U_k=exp(-ik Jz^2/(2j)) exp(-i pi Jy/2)` with k=0 (rigid integrable rotation) and k=6 (commonly used chaotic regime). The two curves share the *same* 100 uniformly oriented spin-coherent initial vectors (seed 20261117).
- q=4 Majorana SYK: N=16 Majoranas; one set of independent real Gaussian quartic couplings with standard deviation `sqrt(6/N^3)`; even-fermion-parity sector d=128; 100 random four-particle Slater determinants in eight complex modes (seed 20261119), which are physical fermionic generalized coherent states. This is **not** the earlier note's full-d=256 vacuum-state run; symmetry-sector volumes and initial ensemble differ.
- Three-site periodic Bose--Hubbard model: eight bosons, d=45; hopping 1 and on-site interaction 0.8; 100 SU(3) coherent states formed by placing all bosons in a random single-particle mode (seed 20261120).
- One real-symmetric Gaussian orthogonal ensemble (GOE) Hamiltonian in the same d=41 spin basis (seed 20261121), evolved from exactly the same spin-coherent initial vectors as the kicked tops.

The SYK, Bose--Hubbard and GOE Hamiltonians are each centered and rescaled to unit rms eigenenergy before continuous-time evolution; the kicked tops use integer Floquet kicks. Hence coincident horizontal coordinates **do not** represent calibrated equal physical times, and the random matrix's scale is a choice. Checks: Majorana anticommutators on every computational-basis vector (absolute tolerance 1e-12), Hamiltonian Hermiticity (1e-11), single-state norm and entropy range at all plotted times (1e-10), Slater and boson-coherent normalizations (1e-10), rotation unitarity, and identical initial GOE/kicked-top entropy.

[Observation, not a theorem] In this one finite-size example the integrable kicked-top *ensemble average* is nearly flat, while the k=6, SYK and GOE curves approach high normalized entropies; Bose--Hubbard grows more gradually. For k=0 the near-flat mean is strongly influenced by uniform sampling of spin orientations under a rigid rotation, and individual curves can vary. The ordering and rates are sensitive to dimension, PVM, initial family, spectral scaling and disorder realization. No chaotic/integrable classification of the Bose--Hubbard parameter point or universal entropy-growth relation is established.

[Numerically tested] The updated script passed its existing Hermiticity, anticommutator, normalization and entropy-bound assertions using `py -3.13` with `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1`, `MPLBACKEND=Agg`. For the kicked top, the first-kick Jz probabilities are independent of k because the diagonal Jz twist comes last in U; an exactly uniform coherent-state orientation ensemble would also give the same mean at t=0 and t=1 (the finite sample agrees approximately). This is a measurement-specific obstruction to using that first-step slope as a chaos comparison. Horizontal times for the Hamiltonians remain normalized-time samples, **not** five physical Floquet kicks. None of these finite-time curves establishes integrability or chaos.
